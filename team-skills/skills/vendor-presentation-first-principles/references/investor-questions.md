# Investor questions for step 1

Step 1 asks which investor question a figure answers. The answer decides which version of the figure is useful: a capital-expenditure figure meant to show "how much the business must reinvest" should include every kind of reinvestment, whatever a single filing line contains.

1. **How does it make money?** What it sells, to whom, and where the profit actually is. Profit is often not where the revenue is, so look at segment margins.
2. **How much cash is left for owners?** What remains after all necessary reinvestment and all real costs, per share.
3. **How much capital does growth need, and what return does it earn?** Look at the return on incremental capital, not only the average.
4. **How durable is it?** Margins through a downturn, pricing power, dependence on a few customers or suppliers.
5. **What can go wrong in the balance sheet?** Debt, maturities, interest, off-balance-sheet commitments and lender conditions.
6. **Does management allocate capital well?** Buybacks and the prices paid, acquisitions later written down, insider ownership and insider buying.
7. **Are the numbers honest?** Adjusted against reported figures, recurring "one-off" charges, accounting-policy changes, restatements, late filings and auditor changes.
8. **What does the price already assume?** The growth and margins the price implies, and what would have to happen for them to be wrong.

## Components that often explain vendor differences

When step 4 leaves a gap, these are frequent causes. Check each one against the original source:

- **Stock-based compensation.** Some vendors treat it as non-recurring or place it below operating income. It is a recurring economic cost that shareholders bear through dilution, but it is not a cash outflow. State which view a definition takes.
- **Reinvestment scope.** Property and equipment only, or also capitalized software and intangibles. Check whether a filer already includes software in its property-and-equipment line before adding lines together.
- **One-off items.** Restructuring, impairments, litigation, and each vendor's own method for identifying them.
- **Interest.** Gross or net of interest earned, and whether capitalized interest is included.
- **Cash.** Whether restricted cash is included.
- **Per-share bases.** Stock splits applied inconsistently across periods.
- **Off-balance-sheet obligations.** Leases, pensions and purchase commitments, included or excluded from debt.
